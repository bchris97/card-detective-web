Grading Guide

PSA grading scale explained

Updated November 15, 2026

PSA grades cards from 1 to 10. Here's what each grade means in plain English, what separates a 10 from a 9, and how much the grade matters for value.

The PSA grades at a glance

GradeNameWhat it looks like
10Gem MintVirtually perfect. Four sharp corners, clean edges, full original gloss, no staining, and centering within PSA's tightest limits.
9MintSuperb, with one minor flaw, such as slightly off-center printing, a tiny print spot or a slightly off-white border.
8Near Mint-MintHigh-end, but may show the slightest wear on a corner or two, a minor printing flaw, or slightly off-white borders.
7Near MintSlight surface wear visible on close inspection, light fraying at some corners, or a slightly out-of-focus picture.
6Excellent-MintVisible surface wear or a printing flaw, slight notching on edges, and some loss of gloss.
5ExcellentMinor rounding of corners, more noticeable surface wear, and loss of original gloss.
4Very Good-ExcellentSlightly rounded corners, noticeable surface wear, and possibly light scuffing or a light crease.
3Very GoodSome rounding of corners, obvious surface wear, possibly a light crease, little gloss left.
2GoodClearly rounded corners, obvious surface wear, and possibly several creases.
1.5FairExtreme wear, but the card is complete.
1PoorHeavily worn or damaged, possibly with pieces missing or heavy creasing.

Summarized in our own words. PSA publishes the full definitions, including exact centering limits for each grade, on its grading standards page.

PSA 10 vs PSA 9: why one point matters so much

The difference between a 10 and a 9 can be a single slightly soft corner or borders a little uneven. For many modern cards, a 10 sells for several times what a 9 does, because collectors chase the top grade and recent cards are often pulled from packs in near-perfect condition, so 9s are common.

That's why grading a modern card is a bet on getting the 10. If it comes back a 9 or lower, the slab may be worth little more than the raw card plus the fee. The grading calculator weighs both outcomes for you.

What PSA graders look at

  • Centering: how even the borders are, front and back. It's often what keeps a sharp card from a 10.
  • Corners: sharp points with no white showing, rounding or dings.
  • Edges: no chipping, whitening or roughness, which shows most on dark borders.
  • Surface: no scratches, print lines, dents, stains or creases, and the original gloss intact. Tilt shiny cards under a light to see scratches.

Qualifiers: OC, ST, PD and the rest

Sometimes a card earns a high grade overall but has one specific problem. Instead of lowering the number, PSA can add a qualifier to the label. Qualified cards usually sell for noticeably less than the same grade without one.

OCOff-center
STStaining
PDPrint defect
OFOut of focus
MKMarks, like ink or pencil
MCMiscut

Cards PSA finds trimmed, recolored or otherwise altered don't get a number grade at all.

Half grades

PSA can give a half-point grade, like 8.5, to a card that sits between two grades. The 1.5 (Fair) grade sits between Poor and Good.

How PSA compares with BGS, SGC and CGC

Other companies grade on similar 1 to 10 scales. Beckett (BGS) also prints four subgrades on the label, for centering, corners, edges and surface. A 10 from one company doesn't always sell for the same as a 10 from another, so compare prices for the same grader and grade.

Questions

What is the difference between a PSA 9 and a PSA 10?

A PSA 10 (Gem Mint) is a virtually perfect card: four sharp corners, clean edges, full gloss and centering within PSA's tightest limits. A PSA 9 (Mint) is nearly as good but has one minor flaw, such as slightly off-center printing or a tiny surface mark. For modern cards a 10 often sells for several times a 9.

What does OC mean on a PSA card?

OC stands for off-center. It is a qualifier PSA adds when a card is otherwise in high condition but its centering falls short of that grade's standard. Other qualifiers include ST (staining), PD (print defect), OF (out of focus), MK (marks) and MC (miscut). Qualified cards usually sell for less than the same grade without one.

Is a PSA 8 a good grade?

A PSA 8 (Near Mint-Mint) is a strong grade, especially for older cards, where few copies survive in better shape. For recent cards, which are often pulled straight from packs in top condition, buyers tend to look for 9s and 10s, so an 8 may sell for not much more than the raw card.

Does PSA give half grades?

Yes. PSA can give a half-point grade, such as 8.5, to a card that sits between two grades. PSA 1.5 (Fair) sits between Poor and Good.
